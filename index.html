Abstract

Inverse problems aim to recover an unknown signal from noisy or incomplete measurements. Recent diffusion-based methods, such as Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS), solve these problems by combining generative priors with measurement-consistency updates. However, the strength of measurement guidance is usually manually tuned and fixed by heuristic schedules.

In this project, we formulate diffusion-based inverse problem reconstruction as a sequential decision-making problem. A reinforcement learning agent adaptively controls the measurement guidance strength during diffusion sampling, allowing the reconstruction trajectory to balance generative prior guidance and measurement consistency. Experiments on image restoration tasks show that the learned adaptive policy improves reconstruction quality over the baseline DPS method.

Method

We model the reconstruction process as a Markov decision process. At each diffusion timestep, the state summarizes the current reconstruction status, including the current sample, timestep, and measurement consistency error. The action corresponds to the measurement guidance strength used in the correction step.

After each action, the environment performs one reverse diffusion update followed by a measurement-consistency correction. The policy is trained to select adaptive guidance strengths that improve reconstruction quality. Instead of using a manually designed schedule, the learned policy automatically determines when to increase or decrease measurement guidance during sampling.

Constant Guidance Strength Study

Before training the RL policy, we first study how different constant measurement guidance strengths affect reconstruction quality. The results show that the guidance strength is sensitive: too small guidance fails to enforce measurement consistency, while too large guidance may overfit the degraded measurement or noise. This motivates learning an adaptive guidance policy instead of using a fixed manually tuned value.

Results

On FFHQ motion deblurring, the RL-based adaptive guidance policy improves over the DPS baseline, increasing PSNR from 25.29 to 29.64, SSIM from 0.77 to 0.84, and reducing LPIPS from 0.25 to 0.16. The learned guidance schedule varies over diffusion time and exhibits a sigmoid-like shape.

We further evaluate different sampling budgets and observe that the RL model consistently outperforms the baseline across different numbers of diffusion steps. With only 600 sampling steps, the RL method achieves comparable performance to the standard 1000-step baseline, suggesting improved inference efficiency.

Task Comparison

We evaluate the proposed policy on multiple restoration tasks, including motion blur, Gaussian blur, random inpainting, box inpainting, and super-resolution. The RL policy generally improves reconstruction quality over the DPS baseline, with less consistent improvement on the box inpainting task.
